## For your judgment

The rules cannot decide these points:

- **Analyst workload.** The manual review queue grows from 555 to 1,200 transactions over the 14-day holdout (about 40 → 86 per day). The share of fraud per reviewed case goes from 28.6% to 26.9%, and block-or-review now catches 92.6% of fraud (was 75.3%). **Can the team absorb this queue?**
- **Still weak after retraining.** The challenger blocks less than 40% of fraud in: tipo_estabelecimento = E-commerce (34.6% of 451 frauds), hour = 21 (0.0% of 121 frauds), hour = 20 (0.0% of 111 frauds), device_type = tablet (38.3% of 60 frauds). Promotion does not fix these; consider a targeted rule or features for them.
- **Customer-impact budget is binding.** Cost alone would block at 0.02 (6.7% of honest customers on validation). The budget (champion's 2.5% + 0.5 pts) keeps it at 0.25. Loosening the budget would catch more fraud and block more honest customers: a business decision about the assumed cost of a false block.
- **Scores are not calibrated probabilities.** A cost-optimal threshold this low means the challenger gives new fraud patterns very low scores while still ranking them well. Decisions use thresholds, so this is safe, but dashboards showing raw scores will read low. Calibration is in the backlog.
- **Evidence size.** The holdout is 14 days (20,079 transactions, 1,142 frauds), with labels at least 30 days old. Segment results with fewer than 30 frauds are shown for information only.
